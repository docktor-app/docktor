**Implements:** [docktor-app/docktor#23](https://github.com/docktor-app/docktor/issues/23). Gap closure for 14-UAT.md gaps G-14-1 and G-14-1b (test 1, major). Root cause: `.planning/debug/g-14-1-stalled-dockerd-no-health-history.md`.

<objective>
Make a stalled Docker daemon produce the health history the product promises, and keep Docktor responsive while it is stalled. Keep database connections alive and bounded, count probe failures that arrive while a database read fails, retry history writes that fail, and bound the remaining unbounded Docker calls.

Purpose: during the UAT stall, the failed probe was never persisted for three stacked reasons: new database connections need Docker's embedded DNS (served by the frozen dockerd) because the pg pool reaps idle connections after 10 s; the failure counter only moved after a successful database read and nothing retried; and, by design (D-08), one failed probe changes nothing. The report that "a frozen dockerd freezes all of Docktor" was refuted as a logging effect and traced to database unreachability plus Docker calls with no timeout outside the probe path.

Spec amendment (developer decision, see the first truth): the expected behavior is an `unhealthy` history entry after 3 consecutive failed probes (a stall of about 70 to 100 s), not an entry for a single failed probe. This plan states it here and in the human-verification wording below, replacing the UAT G-14-1 truth.

Out of scope by decision: logging changes (mechanism refuted), and any change to the 3-failure threshold or to what a single failed probe persists.

Output: a pool-config module wired into `db.ts`, a failure-tolerant `ServiceHealthService`, a history-write outbox with a `createdAt` pass-through, a two-instance `DockerodeClient`, and tests for each, including a stall scenario over a real event bus.
</objective>
