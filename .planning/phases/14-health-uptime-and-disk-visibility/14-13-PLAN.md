**Implements:** [docktor-app/docktor#23](https://github.com/docktor-app/docktor/issues/23) — the probe engine's lifecycle and the success criterion 4 proof: probes that are removed or whose container stops never leave stale health behind, broken probe blocks surface as explained failures, probing load stays bounded, and probe-driven health reaches NotificationWatcher exactly like Docker-driven health.

No SPEC.md exists for this phase, so no probe-derived predicates were generated; requirements come from the GitHub issue with decisions locked in CONTEXT.md (including the 2026-10-07 Post-Research Amendments).

<objective>
Make the probe engine 14-06 delivers safe to live with. Turning a probe off, stopping its container or breaking its YAML must always leave the service with a truthful health. The behavior must also be proven through the real bus to be indistinguishable from Docker health for notifications.

Purpose: Without this plan:
- A removed probe block leaves its last probe health on the service forever (RESEARCH Pitfall 4).
- A typo in the block silently disables the probe, and the service looks healthy.
- Large stacks can open unbounded simultaneous probes.
D-07/D-08 make the probe the service's sole, fail-closed health signal, and #23 criterion 4 requires ERROR/UNHEALTHY notifications to keep working exactly as today.

Re-slice note (2026-10-07 revision): this plan is 14-06's former Task 2, moved verbatim. 14-06's combined estimate (125k) exceeded the 100k smart-zone budget. 14-06 is the tracer slice of the probe engine; this plan is its expansion. 14-09 follows and depends on the lifecycle behavior added here: transitional skip, invalid specs owning the service, and the last-good parse cache.

Output: lifecycle, fail-closed and concurrency changes to the probe job, `handleProbeCleared` on the service, the cleared subscription, and unit, preservation and integration tests.
</objective>

## Trust Boundaries

| Boundary | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| compose file on disk → HealthProbeJob | `x-docktor` values (including broken or removed blocks) can be hand-edited by anyone who can write the stack directory, so they are untrusted |
| Docker Engine API → ServiceHealthService | the Docker health restored on probe removal comes from the local daemon (root-equivalent trust, as today) |
| domain event bus → ServiceHealthService / NotificationWatcher | in-process; payloads come from Docktor's own job and service |
